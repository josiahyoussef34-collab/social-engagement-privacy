Privacy Policy

Version 1.0 · Effective date: 25 October 2026 · Last updated: 25 October 2026

This policy explains how the Social Engagement Control Centre, a private software tool for reviewing social media posts and preparing responses to them, handles information: what it collects, why, who it goes to, how long it is kept, and how you can ask for access, correction or deletion.

1. Summary

  • The software is a private project developed and operated by Josiah Youssef. It is not a public app or website and nobody can sign up to it.
  • I use it for business social media accounts I manage. It reads recent public posts, and some public comments on them, from the LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram business accounts of organisations on a customer list kept for those accounts (Facebook only once Meta approves access).
  • It helps me decide whether to like, comment on or ignore each post. I approve every public action, and today those actions are almost always carried out by hand.
  • When I ask for suggestions, post content is sent to OpenAI's AI service. Commenters' usernames are replaced with neutral labels first (see section 6 for the limits). I also use Anthropic's Claude AI assistant for research and to maintain the software.
  • Information is stored on one computer in New Zealand. It is not sold or used for advertising.
  • I have set retention targets, but they are not yet enforced automatically (see Retention).
  • You can ask for access, correction or deletion. See Your privacy rights and the Data deletion page.

In this policy, "I", "me" and "my" mean Josiah Youssef.

2. Who is responsible

I, Josiah Youssef, developed the software, operate it and am responsible for the information it processes. I decide how it is used, hold the information it collects and answer privacy requests about it. You can contact me as set out in Contact and complaints.

3. What the software does

The software helps me keep up with, and respond naturally to, the social media activity of organisations that the business accounts I manage already work with. Each week it:

  1. takes a copy of the customer list and the links to those organisations' LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram accounts;
  2. reads each organisation's recent posts (roughly the last ten days) and checks, where it can, whether those business accounts have engaged with that organisation recently;
  3. picks at most one post per organisation on each platform for review;
  4. when I allow it, asks an AI service to suggest whether to comment, like or skip, and to draft possible comments;
  5. shows me the post and the suggestion, and records the decision and exact wording I approve; and
  6. keeps a record of what was approved and, where known, what was done.

The software runs on one computer and is opened in a web browser on that computer. Only I use it.

This policy covers the software and related work: research, review, AI-assisted drafting, approval, record-keeping, troubleshooting and backups. It also covers this website. It does not cover how LinkedIn, Meta (Facebook and Instagram) or other platforms handle information.

The software is built to review business posts, not to profile individuals. Some of what it collects is still personal information, such as a person's name in a caption, a face in a photo or a username next to a comment. Most of it is collected indirectly, from social media platforms rather than from you.

The software does not ask for, store or export platform passwords.

The software uses no analytics, advertising identifiers, tracking pixels or third-party cookies.

5. How information is used

I use information only to manage engagement from the business accounts I look after:

  • finding recent posts from organisations on the customer list and reviewing them;
  • suggesting whether to comment, like or skip, and drafting possible comments;
  • avoiding duplicate or too-frequent engagement, and keeping comments varied and natural;
  • recording each decision, the exact approved wording and what was done;
  • checking that the right account acts on the right post, and preventing unapproved actions; and
  • troubleshooting and improving the software.

I do not sell information, use it for advertising, build marketing lists of individuals, or use it to make decisions that have legal or similar effects on people. I do not use it to train AI models.

6. Artificial intelligence

Two AI providers are involved, in different ways. No other AI provider is used. I will update this policy before adopting another.

OpenAI: suggestions made by the software

When I authorise it, for one post or for a weekly batch with a set maximum number of calls, the software sends a request to OpenAI's API (OpenAI is based in the United States). Nothing is sent when posts are only collected or viewed. For each post, the request contains:

  • the post's text, account name, platform, date and media type, and at most one image (sent at low detail, never a video);
  • the text of visible public comments on the post, where collected, with every commenter replaced by a neutral label such as "Commenter 1". @mentions of people inside comments are replaced the same way. A name typed into a comment as ordinary words, or named in the post's own caption, is sent as written;
  • the organisation's other recent posts, earlier comments from the business accounts I manage (to that organisation and some to others), and the comments already planned that week; and
  • engagement guidelines and a summary of how I have edited earlier suggestions.

The software asks OpenAI not to store the response (store: false). OpenAI states that API data is not used to train its models unless the customer opts in, and that it may keep API data in abuse-monitoring logs for up to 30 days, or longer where required by law. I do not have a zero-data-retention arrangement with OpenAI. API use is covered by OpenAI's Services Agreement for business and developer customers. These are OpenAI's published terms, which it can change; see OpenAI's privacy policy and API data controls.

Anthropic: an AI assistant I use

I use Claude, an AI assistant from Anthropic (based in the United States), to help with social media research and review sessions and to develop and maintain the software. This is separate from the software's own AI requests. While doing this work Claude can read posts, comments, working files and local copies of the software's records, which Anthropic then processes. I use Claude on Anthropic's Pro plan, which is covered by Anthropic's consumer terms. Under those terms, Anthropic may use conversations to improve its models if the account's model-improvement setting allows it, and may then keep them for up to five years; if the setting does not allow it, Anthropic generally deletes them within about 30 days of their deletion from the account (longer for content flagged for safety review). The assistant also keeps session records on the computer for about 30 days by default. See Anthropic's privacy policy.

Human oversight

AI output is advice only. It cannot approve or carry out any action. I review every suggestion, can edit or reject it, and make the decision. The AI's original text, my edits and the approved wording are stored separately. Automated checks flag suggestions that claim things the post does not support or that copy the caption, and the AI is told exactly what image it received.

10. Security

I take reasonable steps to protect information from loss, misuse and unauthorised access. I do not claim any security certification. The software's safeguards today:

  • It runs on one computer and accepts connections only from that computer, not from the internet or local network.
  • The database, images, screenshots, working files, browser profiles and credentials stay on that computer and are kept out of the source-code repository.
  • Access keys are kept in a local configuration file and are designed never to appear in the database, logs or screen.
  • Research is read-only: the Meta connection can only make read requests, and the research browser cannot click, type or submit.
  • No public action can start without a recorded approval of that exact action, and key records (approved plans, approvals and action receipts) cannot be silently changed.
  • Commenters' usernames are replaced with neutral labels before anything is sent to OpenAI, and Facebook commenters' names are not stored.

Limitations: the software has no separate sign-in, so access depends on access to the computer and its user account. It does not encrypt its own database or backups, so protection at rest relies on the computer's drive encryption (Windows BitLocker device encryption, switched on for the system drive). Backups are currently kept on the same computer, and LinkedIn research and LinkedIn actions use the same signed-in browser profile.

If a privacy breach causes or is likely to cause serious harm, I will notify the Office of the Privacy Commissioner and the affected people as soon as practicable, as the Privacy Act 2020 requires. I will also notify Meta of any security incident affecting data obtained from Meta, as its Platform Terms require.

11. Retention

Information should be kept only as long as it is needed. These are my retention targets:

InformationTarget period
Other people's comments and usernames collected from posts90 days
Saved images of posts180 days
Posts and AI suggestions1 year
Decisions, approved plans and records of public actions (including the posts they relate to)2 years
Screenshots, working files and logs90 days
Backups and older database copies90 days
Records of privacy requests (which do not contain the deleted information)2 years

How this works today. Nothing is deleted automatically. The software includes a tool that reports how much of each kind of information is held and how much is older than its period. Once I approve the schedule in the software, other people's comments collected by the Instagram check and saved images older than their periods can be removed with that tool, one category at a time after I confirm. Until then the tool only reports. Everything else, including backups, screenshots and logs, is deleted by hand. I have not yet worked through all existing records against these targets, so some information older than them may still be held until I do. Automatic expiry would need further changes to the software and is not in use.

The browser profiles keep sign-in sessions and cached copies of pages they opened until these expire or are cleared. Information held by OpenAI and Anthropic is kept under their terms (see section 6).

12. Deletion

Anyone can ask me to delete information the software holds about them or their organisation. You do not need to have used the software. Email your request to josiahyoussef34@gmail.com with whatever helps me find the information, such as a post link or username. The Data deletion page explains what I do, what the software's deletion tool can and cannot do, and what may need to be kept.

Members of the public never sign in to the software, with Facebook, Instagram or otherwise, so it does not appear in your Facebook or Instagram settings. Please contact me directly instead.

13. Your privacy rights

Under the New Zealand Privacy Act 2020 you can:

  • ask for access to personal information I hold about you;
  • ask for correction of information you think is wrong, or ask me to attach a statement of the correction you wanted; and
  • complain to me, and then to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner.

I will respond to access and correction requests as soon as reasonably practicable and within 20 working days, as the Act requires, unless I extend that time as the Act allows and tell you why. I may ask you to confirm your identity, but only as far as needed. Access requests are free.

The Act does not give a general right to deletion, but I consider every deletion request, and I delete data obtained from Meta on request as Meta's Platform Terms require.

This policy is one of the ways I tell people about information collected from sources other than them, as information privacy principle 3A expects. If you are outside New Zealand, for example in Australia, local privacy law may give you other rights where it applies. Contact me and I will tell you whether, and how, it does.

14. Children, sensitive information and platforms

The software is not directed at children and does not seek information about them or sensitive information such as health, ethnicity, religion or political views. Business posts occasionally include it (for example a photo at a community event, or a post about a safety incident). It is used only to review that post. If you think the software holds such information about you or your child, contact me and I will review and, where appropriate, delete it.

LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram are run by independent companies with their own privacy policies: LinkedIn and Meta. My use of data obtained from Meta is also subject to Meta's Platform Terms.

This website is a set of static pages hosted on GitHub Pages. It has no cookies, analytics, tracking, forms or accounts. GitHub may record technical information such as visitors' IP addresses; see the GitHub Privacy Statement.
